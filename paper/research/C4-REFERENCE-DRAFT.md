## Reference narrative

An evaluation of run-level evidence must distinguish the integrity of a record from the quality of the decision it describes. A receipt may verify even when a response is unusable, unsupported or wrong. Conversely, a correct-looking response may lack the retained material needed for an independent check. The evaluation therefore treats integrity, evidence completeness, execution controls, answer quality, disclosure and operational cost as related but separate properties. The organizational question is whether these properties can support responsible review; the experiments establish only the technical parts measured here.

The evidence comes from several episodes with different purposes and revisions. Controlled fixtures isolate runtime behavior and known mutations. Live-provider cohorts examine execution and generated answers under the conditions observed during collection. The refund study applies the evidence workflow to published decisions about actual disputes. It is retrospective document review: the decisions contain the outcomes, and no staff member used the resulting packets to authorize or settle a refund. These episodes should inform one design argument without being combined into a single performance score.

The integrity tests show why the threat model matters. Defined changes to committed content and signatures were rejected under trusted keys. Retained-file checks also detected missing pages and mismatched requests. These findings depend on the evidence and expectations supplied to verification. Repeating a valid receipt is accepted without a replay cache, and removing an open chain's tail is not reliably detected without an expected count or enforced closure. The historical attack total excludes repeat presentations; reporting the accepted repeats alongside the rejection count makes that boundary visible.

A generic signed canonical log matched Lattice's decisions in the common-record comparison. This is consistent with both using established cryptographic mechanisms. Lattice's design contribution concerns producing and checking evidence within the execution workflow, with explicit commitments and failure outcomes. It does not establish a new signature property or superiority over every secured logging system. The scripted wrong-but-signed control makes a related limit concrete: technical verification can preserve a recommendation that separate business-rule checks reject. Provider honesty, original-source truth and actual external actions still require other evidence.

The refund results illustrate the distinction between signature validity and a complete evidence package. Every terminal signature verified, including the failed response's signature. The malformed response lacked a successful-output commitment, so it did not count as a complete successful-output package. Source-based rubric performance is another measure again. The separately disclosed source audit preserves its corrections and the source decision's inconsistencies rather than changing the original outputs. Those findings support careful record inspection, but they do not show that managers resolve disputes faster or reach better decisions.

Answer-quality evidence also requires several denominators. A response can satisfy the output format while citing an irrelevant source, omit a necessary citation, or make an unsupported claim. The later free cohort reports format, usable output, available judgments, judged correctness, strict source support and abstention separately. The judge is another model, without human calibration. Its results are exploratory. Because paired prompt-only and contract requests had identical bodies and the contract operated after generation, observed differences between generated answers do not demonstrate that format enforcement improved generation. Empty citation lists and unavailable answers are not automatically justified abstentions.

Operational cost must be interpreted with equal care. Controlled signed and unsigned runs provide historical timing distributions under a mocked provider. Their median difference is not a paired-effect confidence interval. Hosted HTTP time combines network and remote service activity, while residual time includes harness work as well as runtime processing. Small free-tier samples do not establish production latency. Receipt size excludes the retained content store, and integration, retention administration and reviewer effort remain unmeasured.

The evaluation therefore supports a bounded conclusion: the implementation produces and checks specified evidence under measured conditions, while its organizational usefulness remains to be tested. A future practitioner study should examine investigation accuracy, review effort and adoption work after the necessary institutional and author decisions. Until then, technical success and an explicit study proposal should be reported on their own terms.
