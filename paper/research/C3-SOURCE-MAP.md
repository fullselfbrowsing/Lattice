## Requirements, criteria and existing evidence

The normative sources motivate needs. Secure-logging and audit literature motivates verifiable-record mechanisms; signatures, content commitments and deterministic routing are our design interpretations. The manuscript's existing Felzmann et al. privacy/transparency and Sculley/Amershi engineering references remain supporting context, not evidence of guaranteed privacy or low adoption effort. We removed the unsupported assertion that high-cost accountability controls are necessarily bypassed.

## Mechanism checks

- [Receipt creation](../../packages/lattice/src/receipts/receipt.ts) assembles commitments, applies redaction and signs canonical bytes. The key is supplied; unconfigured signing is not promised.
- [Public agent wrapper](../../packages/lattice/src/agent/runtime.ts) bypasses receipt minting without a signer, commits successful output only, and reports or throws signing failures. Crew accounting has different coverage; the manuscript retains those distinctions.
- [Verifier options](../../packages/lattice/src/receipts/types.ts) and [verification](../../packages/lattice/src/receipts/verify.ts) distinguish an optional commitment from an explicitly required expected binding. Default signature verification does not establish every required evidence item.
- [Materialization](../../packages/lattice/src/replay/materialize.ts) verifies before calling its artifact loader. This does not authenticate the provider or prevent side effects on every unrelated application path.
- The current [C2 harness](../../eval/public-refund/workflow.mjs) separately checks exact pages, outputs, requests, bindings and source-based grades. Its full inputs are retained locally; no approved-payment workflow is implied.

## Research-method interpretation

The existing DSRM reference permits different entry points and nonsequential activity order ([Peffers et al., DSRM process-model discussion of entry points, author-hosted copy](https://scholar.cgu.edu/samir-chatterjee/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2013/07/jmis-article.pdf)). The revised table organizes recorded activities; it does not invent a prospectively prescribed lifecycle. The new source mapping is explicitly retrospective.

FEDS distinguishes formative/summative purposes and artificial/naturalistic settings ([Venable et al., author manuscript](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/bitstream/handle/20.500.11937/41727/203558_203558.pdf?sequence=2), sections on evaluation dimensions and Technical Risk & Efficacy, journal pp. 80–83). The author-repository copy was accessible through an indexed URL during planning although its clean URL returned 403. Controlled fixture tests, developer-run live-provider experiments and the retrospective public-record review offer technical evidence. Real providers or real published inputs alone do not establish naturalistic organizational use. Practitioner evaluation remains future work. No new empirical episode, participant data or preregistration is claimed.
