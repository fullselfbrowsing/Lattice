## Timing definitions and uncertainty

The controlled [collector](../../eval/scripts/01-overhead.mjs) times direct adapter execution, unsigned runtime execution, signed runtime execution and receipt verification sequentially for each configuration. It also records separate receipt-creation/verification/CID microbenchmarks. Reporting the difference of signed/unsigned p50 values does not compute a paired-effect distribution. No confidence interval for that difference is reconstructed from summary quantiles. The old retained-heap estimate is GC-sensitive and is not treated as durable storage cost.

The free [collector](../../eval/realworld/scripts/14-validated-live.mjs) records original elapsed wall time, HTTP duration, pacing and backoff. Its residual is `max(0, elapsed - HTTP - backoff - pacing)`. The [free report](../../eval/realworld/scripts/18-free-report.py) subtracts pacing from reported wall times and selects only non-warmup cases with runtime success and the expected answer. The published `providerMs` value is **HTTP time**, not isolated model computation. Harness hashing, ledger bookkeeping and JSON work remain inside the residual, including in the direct-HTTP arm.

The new presentation reuses the frozen per-arm p50, p95 and 95% bootstrap median intervals. Those intervals were generated with 10,000 within-arm resamples by the existing report and are not recomputed by C4. The presentation independently cross-checks saved p50/p95 and eligible counts against raw rows. CPU and OS are absent from the free cohort's environment record; they are explicitly unknown rather than borrowed from the controlled host. All original interval values, including HTTP/wall intervals not shown in the compact table, remain in [c4-evidence.json](../c4-evidence.json).

Subtracting separate arm medians cannot isolate signing overhead under variable remote service conditions. Small free-tier samples and descriptive percentiles do not establish a production latency bound or an organizational acceptance threshold. Missing paid timings remain missing.

## Threat model and quality boundaries

The historical [tamper collector](../../eval/scripts/03-tamper.mjs) defines its aggregate using `expectReject && category !== "replay"`. Its repeat-presentation rows retain `expectReject: true` but are accepted and excluded from the aggregate. C4 preserves this historical definition and displays the accepted repeats explicitly rather than silently adjusting the denominator. Intact controls are also separate. Chain tests reject missing middle links and foreign splices; tail truncation needs an expected count or enforced closure. Array reordering is not itself a structural chain mutation. Released 1.6.0 verifier behavior remains explicitly different from newer closure checks.

The matched-record probes use identical record fields and the same signing key for both signed arms. Their parity is evidence for correct implementation of common cryptographic properties, not novelty of the underlying signature primitive or superiority over append-only logging. Integrity still requires a trusted key, and expected-context checks require caller-supplied expectations. A dishonest issuer can sign an inaccurate record. Retaining a hash does not establish full capture, provider consumption, original-document authenticity, human authority or actual external effects.

The quality table keeps six measures distinct: response format, usable output, judge coverage, judged answer correctness, strict source support and judge-labelled abstention. All columns retain 60 planned questions per arm. Strict support requires a non-abstaining answer judged correct with nonempty claims and every claim supported/cited, with relevant citations. Missing or invalid judgments remain unknown. Empty citation lists, filtered outputs and unavailable responses are not relabelled as justified abstentions. All 120 completed prompt/contract request pairs have identical hashes; the contract is a post-generation check, so differences in generated quality do not demonstrate a causal improvement from enforcement. The fixed free judge is not calibrated against human labels. Its probe checks, replacement of the rate-limited judge and scheduling provenance remain in the detailed report.
